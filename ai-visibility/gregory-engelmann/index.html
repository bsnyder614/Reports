What the Audit Found

Four plain-language findings, then three moves to make in order.

Finding 01

Strong when found, invisible when searched.

When someone already knows your name, AI confirms you fast. Your NMLS number, your Highlands bio, and your Experience.com page all line up. But when a Columbus buyer asks ChatGPT for the best loan officer near them, your name doesn't come up, and that's true in Dublin, Hilliard, and the rest of the metro too. Discovery is at 3%. For someone with 20 years and more than 3,500 closings, that gap is the whole opportunity.

Finding 02

Your reviews are great, but they live in the wrong places.

You have 257 reviews on Experience.com with a 4.98 rating, and 99% of them are five stars. That's elite. The trouble is where AI looks. A Google Maps lookup found no listing under your own name. Your Google reviews sit inside the company listing (still titled "Equitable Mortgage" from before the move to Highlands), mixed in with more than a thousand reviews for your teammates. Two of your Dublin teammates have their own Google listings with 64 and 134 reviews. A Zillow profile for you wasn't confirmed. AI can't credit you for reviews it can't tie to your name.

Finding 03

AI sees a few versions of you.

Your old Equitable Mortgage page is still online with a Snouffer Road address and an eqfin.com email. Your Experience.com profile lists your office in Allen, Texas. Your Highlands page lists Dublin with a 941 office number. Some sites call you Greg and some call you Gregory. A search for your name also pulls up a different mortgage person with a look-alike name. When the facts don't match, AI gets less sure it's talking about one person, and it holds back.

Finding 04

Your bio doesn't say what you're known for.

Your Highlands page lists ten specialties, from first-time buyers to renovation loans, and the rest is company text. Your biggest facts ($750 million in personal production, 3,500+ closings, licensed in Ohio and Florida) only show up on your old page. Your reviews keep saying the same things: you explain every option, you answer on weekends, and you close on time. AI needs one clear message to repeat, and right now it has a long list instead.

Your Edge

You already have one: 257 near-perfect reviews, two decades in Columbus, and clients who say they've worked with you for over ten years. You're also licensed in both Ohio and Florida, which is rare and useful for Central Ohio families buying a second home down south. The raw material is stronger than almost anyone in this market. The work now is moving it where AI can see it and saying it the same way everywhere.

04 · How AI Picks

Understanding the Machine

How AI Decides Who to Recommend

When someone asks an AI app for the best loan officer in Columbus, it does two things at once.

1. What it already learned

The app was trained on huge parts of the internet, so it has an idea of who you are if enough places talk about you. Right now AI knows Greg Engelmann is a loan officer with great reviews. It doesn't yet know what you're the go-to person for in Columbus.

2. What it finds right now

It searches the web, reads many pages, and pulls what they say about you into one answer. Then it looks for agreement. Do lots of websites say the same thing about you?

You don't win by writing one great page about yourself. You win when the whole internet says the same true thing about you.

The Three Engines

Everything in this playbook drives one of three engines, and if you skip one the machine stalls.

🔍
Be Found
AI searches the web to answer. If you're not in the results, you're not in the answer, so the older kind of Google work still matters.
✍️
Be Quotable
Write your pages in a clear, simple way AI likes to copy: short lines, real numbers, and true stories from your Columbus clients.
💬
Be Talked About
How much the web talks about you, and whether it's good: reviews, listings, local news, partner pages. It's the strongest engine and the one almost nobody works on.

Layer 1 · Your Service

Layer One

Your Loans & Service

AI doesn't read your rate sheet. It reads your reviews, and so does every buyer who asks about you. You've already earned the reviews, so the job here is getting new ones to land where AI looks.

What Helps

  • Keep reviews fresh. A few new ones each month on Google beat a big pile of older ones somewhere else.
  • Ask every borrower the same way after every close, with your own Google review link.
  • Spread them out: your own Google listing first, then Zillow, then Experience.com.
  • Ask for details, like "Greg found us a VA loan and closed in three weeks," instead of just stars.
  • Reply to every review so AI sees you're real and active.
  • Make sure your loan types show up by name: FHA, VA, jumbo, renovation, investment, second homes.
Compliance note: Never pay for reviews or give gifts in exchange for them, and never post fake ones. Ask honestly and make it easy.
Layer 2 · Your Brand

Layer Two

Your Name & Brand

Ask AI about a loan officer and it will tell the buyer what that person stands for. If you're known for a list of ten things, AI has nothing sharp to say, so it says someone else's name.

Pick Your Lane

Based on your reviews, your licenses, and 20 years in Columbus, here are your strongest options:

  • The veteran who makes first-time buying simple. Your reviews keep saying you explain every step. With 3,500+ closings, you can own "Columbus first-time buyers" with real proof.
  • The Ohio lender for Florida homes. You're licensed in Ohio and Florida. Central Ohio families buying a second home or retiring south need exactly that, and very few local lenders can say it.
  • The lender clients keep for life. Clients say they've worked with you for over a decade. Move-up buyers and repeat refinances are a lane built on trust you already have.

Then Say It the Same Way Everywhere

Your LinkedIn, your Highlands bio, your Google listing, and the way partners describe you should all land on the same one thing. Saying it the same way is the signal, and drifting is what hurts you.

Quick test: If you asked a past client, a Realtor partner, and an AI app "what is Greg Engelmann known for?", would all three give the same answer? If not, this is the fix.

Layer 4 · Your Own Pages

Layer Four

Your Own Pages

All that attention is wasted if there's nowhere good to send the buyer. Your own pages are still some of the most-quoted sources in an AI answer. The goal is for AI to name you and link to your page, which gets you the mention and the click.

Write the Way AI Likes to Quote

  • Answer first. Open with one plain line: who you help, where, and how long you've done it.
  • Break it up. Short lines and clear headings, with no giant blocks of text.
  • Add a Q&A. Real buyer questions with short answers, like "Can an Ohio lender help me buy in Florida?"
  • Use real numbers. 20 years, 3,500+ closings, typical close times, Columbus market facts.
  • Tell real stories. A buyer you closed in three weeks, or a client you've helped for a decade.
  • Show your credentials. Photo, NMLS #305999, and both state licenses, up top.

Layer 5 · Getting It Done

Layer Five

Getting It Done

Plans like this usually fail because of the everyday hassle of doing them. Here are four friction points and how to beat them.

  • Time. You close loans and don't run a marketing team, so build a small weekly routine you'll keep up.
  • Approval. Some posts need a compliance check. Get a few templates approved once so you aren't re-checking every post.
  • Company rules. Know which pages you can change yourself and which need a request to your marketing team.
  • Sticking with it. A fixed page can show up in AI answers within days, but reviews and reputation take months, and the people who win keep going past week three.
Your edge: You've closed more than 3,500 loans, which means you already know how to repeat a process until it works. This is one more process.
06 · Keep Score

Measure It

If You Can't See It, You Can't Win It

1. Ask Where They Came From

Add one question to your forms and your first calls: "Where did you first hear about me?" When someone says they found you on ChatGPT, you'll know AI is sending you business.

2. Do a Monthly AI Check

Once a month, open each AI app fresh and run your key questions. It takes about 20 minutes and it's free.

  • Open ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Google AI, and Claude in fresh windows.
  • Ask "Best loan officer in Columbus OH," "Best mortgage lender in Dublin OH," and your lane questions.
  • Note whether you showed up, where, and what it said about you.
  • Note which websites the AI used. Those are your next targets for Layer 3.
  • Track the trend month over month, since direction is what matters.

08 · Rules to Follow

Toolkit B

Rules to Follow

AI visibility is marketing, and you work in a regulated field. This is general guidance, not legal advice, so check your plans with your compliance team.

AreaWhat to Keep in Mind
NMLS LicenseShow your NMLS number where required. Keep your name, company, and license the same everywhere, and only advertise where you're licensed (Ohio and Florida).
RESPADon't pay, or get paid, just for referrals. Shared ads with agents or builders have to be paid for fairly, for real work. Get these reviewed.
Loan Ads (TILA)If you list a specific rate or term, you may need more disclosures. Avoid rate claims that sound too good to be true.
Fair LendingAdd "Equal Housing" where it fits. Never target or exclude people based on protected traits.
ReviewsHonest only. Never buy, fake, or pay for reviews, and know that some states have extra rules.
AccuracyKeep numbers, limits, and program details current, since old claims can create liability.
AI-Written ContentYou're responsible for anything posted under your name, even if AI wrote it. Check every number before it goes live.
One rule that covers most of this: Build your name on things that are true, clear, and said the same way everywhere. True keeps you legal, clear is what AI copies, and the same everywhere is what AI trusts.

10 · Glossary

Plain-English Reference

Words to Know

AI search apps
ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, and Google's AI. They answer questions in plain words instead of just showing links.
AI Overview / AI Mode
The AI-written answer at the top of Google, above the normal results.
SEO
Search engine optimization: helping your pages show up high in normal Google results. It's the floor, and it still matters.
AI Visibility
How often, and how well, AI apps mention and recommend you when buyers ask about loan officers in your market.
Zero-Click Search
When someone reads the AI answer and stops without clicking any website. About 8 in 10 AI-answered searches end this way.
Branded Search
When someone searches for you by name. They already know you exist, and your score here is strong.
Discovery Search
When a stranger searches for a kind of help, like "best loan officer near me." This is where your gap is.
Sentiment
The overall feeling, good or bad, the web shows about you, mostly from reviews.
Citation
A website AI used and points to as its source. Being cited means AI trusted your page enough to reference it.
Third-Party Site
Any website that isn't yours, like Zillow, Reddit, or a local news site. AI trusts these more than your own pages.
Google Business Profile
Your free Google listing with your info, map pin, and reviews. It's one of the most important things for local AI search.
NMLS / Consumer Access
The national license system for loan officers. NMLS #305999 is your official ID, and Consumer Access is the public lookup AI uses to check you.
Your Lane / Niche
The one main thing you want to be known for. A clear lane gives AI a reason to name you over a generalist.
Answer-First
Writing a page so the clear answer comes first and the details follow. AI prefers to quote pages written this way.
RESPA
Federal law that bans paying for referrals. It matters most when working with agent partners.
TILA / Reg Z
Federal loan ad rules. If you list a specific rate or term in marketing, you may need to add disclosures.
