Before You Start
Get Yourself & Your Course Approved
The goal: become an approved instructor and turn a presentation like our Non-QM presentation into an Ohio-approved Realtor CE class. Union Home Mortgage is already an approved Ohio CE provider, so you do not need to establish yourself as a separate CE provider.
What Your Course Should Cover

Help Realtors understand self-employed borrowers, real estate investors, Bank Statement loans, DSCR financing, asset-utilization programs, other Non-QM solutions, and how to identify borrowers who may not fit traditional agency guidelines.

Remember This is education, not a UHM sales presentation. Your expertise is the marketing.

Complete the CE Application

Ohio Synchronous CE Course Application

For a live classroom or live webinar presentation, complete the Real Estate Synchronous Continuing Education Application.

The Application Will Ask For
  • UHM's CE Provider File Number
  • Union Home Mortgage as the Course Provider
  • Course title
  • Requested CE hours
  • Initial class date and time
  • Class location
  • Instructor information
  • Type of training
  • Requested CE classification, typically Elective for a course such as Non-QM
Application Fee $50, when submitted at least 45 days before the initial offering
Do This Download the Ohio Synchronous CE Application before starting Step 3 so you can reference it while completing your Instructor Certification.

Complete Your Instructor Certification

Every teaching LO needs this on file

Each LO who wants to teach the course must complete the Real Estate Instructor Certification. This form is included in the current Ohio Synchronous CE application packet.

The Form Requires
  • Your contact information
  • UHM's provider information
  • Course information
  • Your qualifications
  • Professional licensing and legal-history questions
  • Your signature
Easiest Qualification Path For most experienced Loan Officers: "Five years full-time experience in a profession, trade, or technical occupation in the applicable field."
Don't Skip This Ohio specifically requires a résumé or biography showing how you meet the instructor qualifications. Attach it to your submission.

Assemble Your Submission Package

Everything goes in one package before you submit
Before You Submit, Confirm You Have
  • Completed Synchronous CE Application
  • Completed Instructor Certification
  • Résumé or professional bio
  • Timed and detailed course outline / syllabus
  • PowerPoint presentation or course materials
  • Sample attendance / completion certificate
  • Sample advertisement, if you plan to advertise the class
  • $50 application fee
Important Ohio bases CE credit on a 60-minute hour of actual instruction, excluding breaks and other non-instruction time. Build your timed outline accordingly.

Wait for Approval

Don't market it as CE until it's official
The Problem It can be tempting to start promoting your class the moment you submit. Advertising a course as Ohio-approved CE before you have actual approval from the Division puts your course, and your credibility, at risk.
The Solution Hold your marketing until the approval letter is in hand. Once approved, save these for your records:
  • Course certification number
  • Approved course materials
  • Instructor approval
  • Approved number of CE hours

Take It to Your Realtor Partners

Now the real value starts

Once approved, use the course to create value for your Realtor relationships.

Say This

"We've put together an Ohio-approved CE class around financing solutions for borrowers who don't fit neatly into traditional mortgage guidelines. I'd love to bring it to your office and provide the class for your agents."

The Objective The objective isn't to sell UHM during the class. Your expertise is the marketing. Teach something valuable and become the Loan Officer Realtors think of when they encounter a borrower who doesn't fit the traditional mortgage box.

Don't simply learn Non-QM products. Use your knowledge to create a reason to get in front of more Realtor partners.
